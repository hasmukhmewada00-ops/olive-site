Corporate catering in Gandhidham works best when you plan around three things: the format of the event, the dietary mix of your team, and the practical details of the venue. A pure vegetarian menu with Jain options is often the simplest choice for mixed teams, because almost every guest can eat from it.

Gandhidham, together with the port and industrial areas around Kandla and Mundra, hosts a steady calendar of business events. This guide covers how to plan catering for them.

Common corporate catering formats

  • Working lunches and meetings: quick to serve, light enough to keep people alert in the afternoon.
  • Conferences and training days: breakfast, tea breaks with snacks, lunch and evening tea.
  • Launches and client events: a more polished buffet or live counters to make an impression.
  • Annual days, festivals and family days: larger guest counts, often with families and children.

Tell your caterer which format you are planning, the timings, and whether guests will eat standing, seated or at their desks.

Plan the menu around your team

Teams in Kutch often include people from many states, along with guests who eat Jain food or avoid certain ingredients. A few habits make menus work for everyone:

  • Choose a pure vegetarian menu so no guest is left out.
  • Include Jain dishes, clearly labelled, if any guests need them.
  • Mix familiar dishes, such as dal, rice, rotis and a paneer or vegetable curry, with one or two regional specials.
  • Keep lunch on working days lighter than an evening event.

Practical details to confirm

Corporate venues, especially at plants and port areas, often have rules that affect catering. Confirm these with your admin or facilities team and your caterer:

  • Site access: entry passes or vehicle permissions for the catering team.
  • Setup time: when the caterer can arrive, set up and clear away.
  • Space and power: where the buffet will go, and whether power is available for warmers.
  • Documentation: the caterer's FSSAI licence, and a GST invoice if your company needs one.
  • Headcount changes: by when the final number must be confirmed.

Corporate catering checklist

  • Event format, date and timings
  • Expected headcount and the date it will be final
  • Number of Jain guests or other dietary needs
  • Venue access, setup window, space and power
  • Caterer's FSSAI licence and invoicing details
  • Service staff needed during the event
