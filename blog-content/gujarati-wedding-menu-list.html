A Gujarati wedding menu usually moves through welcome drinks, farsan, a main thali-style meal, live counters and sweets. This guide lists menu ideas course by course for a pure vegetarian wedding in Gandhidham or Kutch, and shows how to keep Jain guests in mind without shrinking the menu.

Use it as a starting list for your discussion with the caterer. Final dishes depend on your family's traditions, the season and the function.

1. Welcome drinks and starters

Guests often wait at the entrance while families greet each other, so the first course should be easy to pick up and eat standing.

  • Chilled drinks such as buttermilk, jaljeera or fruit coolers
  • Farsan such as dhokla, khandvi, patra and samosa
  • Paneer or vegetable starters served hot

2. The main meal

The core of a Gujarati wedding meal is a balanced plate: something dry, something with gravy, a lentil, bread and rice.

  • Dal and kadhi: the two dishes guests expect on a Gujarati plate
  • Sabzi: two or three seasonal vegetable dishes, one dry and one with gravy
  • Paneer dish: a richer gravy dish for variety
  • Breads: rotli, puri or parathas, ideally served hot
  • Rice: plain rice, jeera rice, pulao or khichdi
  • Accompaniments: salad, papad, pickle and chaas

3. Live counters

Live counters add variety and let guests choose. They work especially well at receptions and sangeet nights. Olive Catering Company's popular live counters are rumali roti, pani puri and pizza, all made fresh in front of guests. Ask your caterer for enough staff at each counter so queues stay short.

4. Sweets and dessert counter

  • One or two traditional sweets such as shrikhand, mohanthal or jalebi
  • A dessert counter with a few options so guests can choose
  • Ice cream or a cold dessert in warm months

Keeping the menu Jain-friendly

If Jain guests are attending, the easiest approach is to plan Jain versions of several dishes rather than one separate plate. Jain dishes are made without onion, garlic or root vegetables, and they should be cooked and labelled separately on the buffet. Our Jain catering guide explains what to ask your caterer.

Menu planning tips

  • Plan by function. The mehndi lunch, the wedding dinner and the farewell breakfast do not need the same menu.
  • Do not repeat dishes across functions that the same guests attend.
  • Balance heavy and light. Pair a rich paneer dish with a simple dry sabzi.
  • Think about season. Choose cold drinks and desserts in summer and warm soups in winter.
  • Ask for a tasting for the main wedding meal if time allows.
