Good wedding catering in Gandhidham starts with three numbers: how many functions you are hosting, how many guests each one will have, and how many of those guests need Jain food. Fix those first and every other decision, from the menu to the number of service staff, becomes much easier.

This guide walks through how to plan wedding catering step by step, what to ask a caterer before you book, and a checklist you can share with your family.

1. List every function, not just the wedding day

A Gujarati or Kutchi wedding is rarely one meal. There may be a mehendi lunch, a sangeet or garba dinner, a haldi breakfast, the wedding lunch and a reception. Each one has a different mood and a different crowd, so plan them one at a time:

  • Function and time: breakfast, lunch, hi-tea or dinner.
  • Guest count: a realistic number, not the invitation count.
  • Style: sit-down, buffet, or buffet with live counters.
  • Venue: banquet hall, farmhouse, lawn or home.

Sharing this list with your caterer early helps them suggest menus that do not repeat across functions and plan the right number of staff for each one.

2. Plan for pure veg and Jain guests from the start

In Gandhidham and across Kutch, many weddings have a mix of guests who eat regular vegetarian food and guests who follow Jain food rules. Jain food is cooked without onion, garlic or root vegetables such as potato and carrot, and many Jain guests prefer to eat before sunset.

The simplest way to handle this is to choose a caterer who cooks pure vegetarian food as standard and can prepare Jain dishes for the same event. 3. Choose the right serving style

The serving style changes how your guests experience the meal as much as the menu does.

  • Buffet: flexible and works for large guest counts. Plan enough counters so queues stay short.
  • Live counters: dishes like dosa, chaat or jalebi made fresh in front of guests. They add energy to the evening and spread guests across the venue.
  • Sit-down service: a more formal meal, usually for smaller functions or close family.

Many families combine a main buffet with two or three live counters for the reception or sangeet.

4. Ask the right questions before you book

Before you confirm a caterer, ask:

  • Is the kitchen FSSAI licensed, and where is the food cooked?
  • How many service staff and supervisors will be at each function?
  • Who handles the buffet setup, counters and serveware?
  • How are Jain dishes prepared and labelled on the buffet?
  • Is a tasting possible before the final menu?
  • What is included in the quote, and what is charged separately?

5. Book early in the wedding season

The busiest wedding months in Gujarat usually run from around November to February, along with popular auspicious dates through the year. Good caterers get booked quickly on those dates, so it is worth contacting caterers as soon as your wedding date is fixed, even if your final guest count is not.

Wedding catering checklist

  • Functions, dates and timings listed
  • Guest count per function, with the number of Jain guests
  • Serving style chosen for each function
  • Live counters shortlisted
  • Venue kitchen and power arrangements checked
  • Caterer's FSSAI licence and staffing confirmed
  • Menu tasting scheduled
  • Final quote and inclusions in writing
