Jain catering means vegetarian food prepared without onion, garlic or root vegetables, often served before sunset. If you are hosting a wedding or function in Gandhidham with Jain guests, the most important step is choosing a caterer who understands these rules and plans for them from the first menu discussion.

This guide explains what Jain food excludes, what to ask your caterer, and how to plan a buffet that works for both Jain and non-Jain guests.

What Jain food does not include

Jain food rules come from the principle of causing the least possible harm to living beings. Practices differ between families, but Jain menus generally leave out:

  • Onion and garlic
  • Root vegetables such as potato, carrot, beetroot, radish, fresh ginger and sweet potato
  • Honey
  • Meat, fish and eggs, as with all vegetarian food

Many Jain guests also avoid eating after sunset, and some families follow stricter rules on certain days, such as avoiding green leafy vegetables during Paryushan. It is always worth asking the hosts or elders which practices matter for your guests.

What a Jain menu can include

A good Jain menu does not feel like a menu with things taken out. Plenty of dishes are naturally Jain or easy to adapt:

  • Dal, kadhi and rice
  • Rotli, puri and parathas
  • Sabzi made with cabbage, capsicum, gourds, beans or raw banana in place of potato
  • Paneer dishes in gravies made without onion and garlic
  • Farsan such as dhokla and khandvi
  • Sweets such as shrikhand, mohanthal and jalebi

Ask your caterer which of their signature dishes they can prepare in a Jain version, so Jain guests are not limited to two or three options.

Questions to ask your caterer about Jain food

  • Do you prepare Jain menus regularly, or only on request?
  • How are Jain dishes kept separate while cooking and serving?
  • How are Jain dishes labelled on the buffet?
  • Can Jain dishes be served before sunset if needed?
  • Can you handle stricter requirements for specific guests or days?

Planning one buffet for Jain and non-Jain guests

Most events in Kutch have both Jain and non-Jain guests. Two approaches work well:

  • Mixed buffet with clear labels: Jain and regular dishes on the same counters, each one clearly marked.
  • Dedicated Jain counter: a separate counter with its own serving spoons, useful when many guests eat Jain food.

Either way, tell your caterer roughly how many guests will eat Jain food so quantities are right for both.
