Pooja and festival catering is about getting the timing, the food rules and the guest flow right. This guide covers what to decide before you book a caterer for a pooja, Navratri gathering or community jamanvar in Gandhidham or across Gujarat.

Start with the food rules

Religious functions often come with rules about what can be served. Settle these first and tell your caterer clearly:

  • Pure vegetarian only. Check that the caterer's kitchen is fully vegetarian. Olive Catering Company is 100% pure vegetarian.
  • Jain rules. If guests follow Jain practice, ask for dishes without onion, garlic or root vegetables.
  • Fasting guests. If some guests are fasting, plan a separate menu and label it clearly.
  • Timing. Some families serve food before sunset. Say so early.

Decide the format

  • Seated jamanvar: traditional and suited to community functions, but needs more serving staff.
  • Buffet: flexible for larger or mixed groups.
  • Packed or delivered meals: useful for small home poojas where cooking at the venue is not practical.

Plan the menu

Poojas and festival lunches usually work best with a short, well-cooked menu: a farsan or starter, dal, kadhi or sabzi, rotli or puri, rice and a sweet. Keep the dishes simple and let quality do the work. Seasonal festival menus, such as for Navratri, can be planned around what your family traditionally serves.

Think about the guest flow

  • How many guests, and when will they arrive?
  • Is there enough space for serving and queues?
  • Who will look after elders and children first?
  • Are there clean-up and waste arrangements? Eco-friendly serveware is available from Olive on request.

What to tell your caterer
